About Me

I am a PhD candidate in Environmental Resources Engineering (concentration: Geospatial Information Science & Engineering) at the State University of New York College of Environmental Science and Forestry (SUNY ESF), advised by Prof. Giorgos Mountrakis. My doctoral research focuses on satellite-based heat hazard modeling and environmental justice and health impact assessment.

My work uses remote sensing and machine learning for local-scale weather and hazard forecasting with uncertainty quantification, covering extreme heat, precipitation, and drought. I integrate satellite data with numerical model outputs and ground observations to assess environmental, agricultural, and public-health impacts. A recent example is SHINE, a 1 km daily heat index model for the conterminous United States, published in the ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing.

Before ESF, I earned an M.Eng. and a B.Eng. in Civil Engineering, specializing in water engineering and hydraulic structures. I collaborate with researchers in Spain, the United Kingdom, Malaysia, and the Middle East, have reviewed more than 50 manuscripts, and serve as Guest Editor of a special issue on intelligent hydro-meteorological systems in Physics and Chemistry of the Earth.
